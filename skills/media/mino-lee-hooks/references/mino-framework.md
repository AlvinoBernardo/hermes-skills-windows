## Why hooks matter
- A hook is the first 3-5 seconds. If 90% of viewers leave before the good part at second 10, the platform never pushes the video. "80% of why a video got 1,000 vs 100,000 views is the hook."
- TikTok and Instagram show a new video to a test group of 300-500 viewers. Too many swipes in the first 3 s (skip rate), or too little engagement, and distribution stops.
- Hashtags, posting times and camera quality are the other 20%.

## Step 1 — Copy (build the bank)
- Scroll TikTok, not Instagram. TikTok's search and For You page are more refined.
- Only save hooks from videos with **50k+ likes** (or 100k+ views) in **your format**: talking head, vlog, montage, list or how-to. Format matters more than niche, so it's fine to study adjacent niches in the same format.
- Write the hooks down word for word. Then combine hook frameworks with your ideas, swapping in your niche's keywords. Example: "6 sales objections in 60 seconds" becomes "6 viral hooks in 60 seconds".
- Copy the visual setup too: the setting (in the car), the text style (classic TikTok white text with a black outline above the head), the pacing and the cadence.
- TikTok and Instagram trail YouTube and Twitter by about 1 week to 1 month. Study outlier YouTube titles (1.3x+ the channel's average, last 6 months, 45k+ views) for upcoming hook angles. Example: "I studied 1,000 hooks, here's how to actually go viral" becomes "I studied 1,000 fitness creators, here's how to actually build muscle."

## Step 2 — Understand (keywords)
- Keywords are **words that trigger emotion**, not SEO terms. Examples: "view jail", "algo pull", "shitty habits", "academic assault rifle", "mental masturbation", "zero motivation scrolling", "dopamine detox", "lock in", "6am", "f*ck-you money", "passive income", "AI agents", "$10k a month", "$2,000 a day".
- Use the words people actually say to friends, not polished phrasing. "Break your shitty habits" beats "develop better habits."
- Keep a bank of niche keywords. YouTube title search is a quick way to find which words are hot.

## Step 3 — Maximize (the checklist he uses with paying clients)
1. **Clarity.** Is this the clearest, most concise wording? Simple words only; anything hard to parse gets swiped.
2. **Specificity and numbers.** Add a result, a timeframe or a dollar figure. "alone" beats "by yourself"; "$10k a month" beats "successful".
3. **Purple cow.** Make it novel. Nobody says "AI agents that make you f*ck-you money in your sleep", and that is why it stops the scroll.
4. **Alignment.** The video must deliver what the hook promises. Rage bait that the video doesn't back up doesn't actually work.
5. **Ideal viewer.** Who exactly are you talking to? Talk to your past self from 3 years ago.
6. **Visuals.** Can a visual make it easier to understand, or more gripping?

Then:
- Write **5-10 variations** of every hook. The first draft is never the best.
- **Read each one aloud 5 times** and keep the one that sounds right.

Worked example from his lesson:
- Draft: "If you're tired of running your business 24/7 by yourself..."
- Final: "This is the ultimate guide to start using AI agents to build a passive income machine. Take notes and thank me later."
- Follow-up line: "thousands of kids just like you are using AI agents to make 10K per month, but you're just too lazy to set it up."

## The 3 first principles (from "I studied 500 hooks")
1. **Demand.** Copy only what has already proven demand (5k+ likes / 100k+ views). Don't trust your own taste until you've earned it.
2. **Value per second.** Viewers subconsciously judge "how valuable is this, and how long will it take?" "6 sales objections in 60 seconds" beats "3 sales objections" and beats having no time stated. Deliver the first real nugget within 5-10 seconds. Cut every backstory sentence that isn't the point.
3. **Social proof.** For education and inspiration, show early why you're qualified. That can be results ("I've grown 4 accounts") or relatability. His second-sentence trick is **"I know you're probably thinking..."**: it signals that he understands the viewer. Pain-point storytelling ("3 years ago I was in the most hideous Hinge addiction of my life, swiping 5 hours a day...") works without any expert credential.

## What he says kills hooks
- AI-written hooks that sound bland and robotic. Being "too original", which ends up boring in the first 5 s.
- No keywords, no clear viewer, a slow start, talking too slowly, no front light, no text on screen, bad audio or music too loud.
- "Day 1 of road to $10k/month" series hooks. "Not a good hook, it doesn't play into why things go viral." The data agrees: 3% hit rate.

## Use AI right
- AI can't write the whole video: "you cannot automate authentic value". It can speed up hooks, because hooks are mostly reworked proven patterns.
- Feed it the full script. Without the script, hooks come out vague and broad.
- Generate 10 variations and rank them least to most viral. Pick the one that is most concise, accurately describes the video, and triggers the most emotion.

## Beyond 100k views (his "giga hook")
- Past ~100k views, gimmicks stop mattering. Charisma, energy, tone and presence decide it.
- The hook should make the viewer feel uniquely heard. The best creators "create from love toward the viewer, not from clawing for attention."
