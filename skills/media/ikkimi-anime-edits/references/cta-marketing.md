## What the evidence says works
- **App first, anime as the payoff** gets the reach. Example: Duolingo-style sentence cards as a setup, then a hard cut into a Blue Lock edit at 11 s (5.0M views). Another: paid skits in 9-11 s with English line -> stiff textbook Japanese -> the real anime line as the punchline (1.7M / 1.8M).
- **Edit/content -> sudden pivot** (the user's literal ask) is accepted only when real value lands before the ask: a slang breakdown, then the app line, got 395K views with "good damn ad" comments.
- **Pure product demos, launch posts and "I wish I found this sooner, App name:" templates flop** (hundreds to a few thousand views).
- Hate is rare. The main friction in the comments is price, so be upfront that it's an ad instead of hiding it.
- The user's chosen promo format is **hook → his own POV demo**; its full recipe, the proven Lucy hooks and his edit preferences are in `references/hook-to-demo.md`.

## Account + legal rules (always on)
- **The edit account (trending songs) never shows the product.** TikTok's music terms forbid tying library songs to a brand, and business accounts lose trending music. Even permissive anime fan-use guidelines ban using clips to advertise your own product. At most a caption credit or pinned comment, on about 1 post in 5.
- **CTA/demo videos go on a separate, disclosed Ikkimi brand account** using the scene's own audio or Commercial Music Library tracks, with the "promotional content" toggle on. Spark Ads only on brand-account posts that already performed, never on anime + trending-song edits.
- Ask which region the TikTok account is registered in before planning a CTA test. The Mac runs on WIB time, so it may be Indonesia, and you want most viewers from the markets Ikkimi sells to.

## Founder face account (AJ the Senpai / Kito Senpai style, research in `~/Desktop/Ikkimi/Ikkimi-2026-11-13-senpai-face-account/`)
- The user wants a spoken, casual CTA at the end ("I'm building an app called Ikkimi that lets you learn Japanese from the anime you watch... if you're an anime fan, check it out, link in bio"), not a hidden hover-only hint. Put it after the value + "so tell me..." question; he is the founder, so "I'm building" doubles as the disclosure.
- Get the show's theme right from HIS reading: he rejected framing A Silent Voice as a romance/misunderstanding (suki/tsuki); to him it's Shoya finding a reason to live. Ask or offer the angle when a show has several readings.
- Don't reuse the reference creator's own hook for the same show (AJ already did Silent Voice at 1.3M).
- **His speaking voice for the story part (he asked to remember this):** casual retelling, short and simple, like telling a friend: "So it's basically about a boy who bullied this girl... like, so bad that she ended up leaving the school... so a few years later he meets the girl again..." Use "so", "basically", "like", plain words, one idea per line. No writerly beats ("The movie literally puts an X over every face"), no symbolism tours, no stacked clever lines. Keep the Japanese explanation to 2-3 plain lines ("which basically means help me live").
- **The user REJECTED Kito Senpai's format ("kito senpai is shit"): use ONLY the AJ the Senpai format** (split screen, "There's an anime where..." paradox hook, opinion, casual story, Japanese line, thesis, format facts, "so tell me...", CTA; 60-75 s; sad/cult shows). Don't suggest Kito skits, couch reactions or yaps. Kito research is archived in `_old/kito/`.
- His CTA wording (keep it verbatim): "Oh yeah, by the way, if you want to learn Japanese while watching anime, you should go check out the Ikkimi app." Link in bio only as on-screen text.

## Creative rules from the fan-panel critique
- A mouse cursor, browser chrome or app name on an anime shot reads as an ad in about half a second. Hide the UI inside the edit's own language (the word flips colour on the existing white flash, freeze 1.2-1.5 s), or put the UI deliberately at 0 s (app-first format) so there's no bait-and-switch.
- Don't teach words every fan knows (大好き, 好き, 来る, どうしよう, どう). Pick a word that unlocks the scene (a particle, a rough ending like ねえ). Exception: when the user explicitly picks the line (e.g. a confession), hover his word and offer a less basic alternative take.
- Textbook-vs-anime skits need a **register jump** in column 3 (polite → blunt/rough, e.g. 今それどうでもいいだろ, 終わってほしくねえな). A common phrase repeated (どうしよう ×3) is no joke, and the fan panel scored it 4.25/10. Never explain the punchline on screen.
- Never put first-person testimonial lines ("i hovered it…") on a brand account's video: it reads as a brand faking a customer. Use one gloss per word across video, reply and pin.
- Real dialogue lines + timings for the Blue Box demo clip `pHJP54XVKPk` are in `~/Desktop/Japanese app/src/demo-clips.json` (tokens = live hover targets). Check lines there instead of guessing.
- Keep everything after the payoff to 3 s or less, and unfreeze into the last chorus word so it loops.
- Fan voice talks about her, the scene or me. Marketer voice talks about you, your laptop or the tool, and gets scrolled.
- Growth counterweight: on the brand account, the viewer must recognise "my real episode on Netflix/Crunchyroll/YouTube" within 2 s, so show the player controls.

## Recording the real UI
- Screen RECORDINGS of Netflix/Crunchyroll come out black (DRM). Filming the laptop with a phone (the user's POV format) avoids that entirely.
- For an agent-made screen recording, use an official YouTube clip that has human Japanese captions (Ikkimi auto-switches to the JP track). The official Netflix Anime Fragrant Flower confession clip `0P1Tg2ovSAM` and the official Blue Box clips work. Fallback: the app's Playwright setup on our own clip + subtitle file.

## Funnel + metrics
- Phone viewers can't install a Chrome extension. Before any CTA post, have a per-video landing page that sends desktop visitors to the store and leads phones with "email me the link for my laptop". Lead with the iPhone app the day it ships (about 4x more trials per view).
- A clickable bio link on a personal TikTok needs about 1,000 followers. Comment-keyword DMs work on Instagram (ManyChat) but not on TikTok in the US/EU/UK.
- North stars: CTA posts = link taps per 1k views (target 1.0 or more, kill below 0.3). Pure edits = follows per 1k views (4 or more). Saves are not a conversion signal.
- Judge CTA posts against the median of the edit account's last 10 pure edits. Keep a format at 70% or more of that, drop it below 50%, and give each variant about 8 posts before calling it.

## Face / personal-brand account (the user on camera)
A third account type next to the edit account and the brand account: the user himself as an anime 'senpai' whose twist is always the real Japanese. Research + plan: `~/Desktop/Ikkimi/Ikkimi-2026-11-13-senpai-face-account/` (PLAN.md, research/ajthesenpai + kitosenpai REPORT.md).
- **Layout + skeleton that wins (AJ the Senpai, YouTube Shorts):** anime clips top ~45%, his face bottom 55% on a plain wall in the same shirt every video; first spoken line "There's an anime where [paradox premise]", opinion line, 3-5 beats, a thesis line, format facts, then "So tell me, what's the anime where...". 60-75 s at ~240 wpm. Sad/cult shows (A Silent Voice, Grave of the Fireflies, 86, Anohana) hit 400K-1.4M; mainstream trivia/lists flop.
- **Japanese voice-actor / dub topics** were Kito Senpai's strongest cluster (1.1M-8.6M: "This Japanese voice actor has an INSANE voice", "The Japanese dub for X is WILD"). His recent couch reactions with the TV in frame (15-26 s, "EVERYONE after...") still pull 10x his median.
- **Avoid:** 2 posts/day with vague near-identical titles and 50+ s plot summaries (Kito's median fell ~7x).
- Ikkimi only appears as the tool on screen when the real JP subtitle is shown; no CTA in the video, link in bio, promo toggle on when the product is visible, never 'free'.
- Pitch format the user accepted: the idea in 2-3 plain sentences, a two-column "what to steal from whom" table with view counts, the beat template, ONE ready hook for video #1, then offer the full scripts.

## Process that produced this (reuse for marketing questions)
Write one BRIEF.md in the research folder. Then run round 1 with 5 parallel agents: competitor teardown with real view counts and outliers, viral-format dissection of saved refs, a concept generator with beat sheets, CTA copy from the mino-lee-hooks bank, and distribution/legal risk. Round 2 runs a concepts rewrite from the evidence, an anime-fan persona panel critic and a performance-marketer critic that verifies product facts in code and stores. Round 3 is one synthesizer. The critics caught facts that changed the plan (iPhone app not live, price, music terms), so never skip the fact-checking critic.
