# Ikkimi anime edits

Ikkimi ("Japanese from your shows", app project `~/Desktop/Japanese app/`) markets with anime. The current phase warms up a TikTok account with fan-style anime edits: **no CTA, no app name, no logo, no watermark**. Library: `~/IkkimiAssets/` (CATALOG.md, research/, footage/, music/, engine/, pipeline/, edits/). Deliverables: `~/Desktop/Ikkimi/Ikkimi-<date>-<name>/`.

## When to Use
Any request to research, plan, render or fix an anime edit for Ikkimi, including "here are references, make one like this". For pure trend scraping (what's viral, which songs), see `references/trend-research.md`. For marketing ideas, CTAs, a promo version of an edit (edit -> app), or a **face/personal-brand account** where the user talks on camera like an anime creator, read `references/cta-marketing.md` first. Its account and legal rules decide which account may show the product at all. For **hook → POV-demo videos** (UGC face hook, then the user's own iPhone footage of Ikkimi on his laptop) and the scene shot lists he films from, follow `references/hook-to-demo.md`. Note that the 16:9-in-9:16 format and the edit-engine procedure below do NOT apply to those videos.

## Always-on rules (user preferences)
- **The user's references define "good".** Model critic scores do not predict his taste: edits scored 8/10 were called bad. Build from the reference edits he sends, and judge drafts by a side-by-side against the reference plus his feedback.
- **One edit learns from all the references.** When he sends several links, analyse each, then make ONE edit led by the main reference. Do not make one remake per link unless he asks.
- **Format:** a 1080x1920 file with the edit as a **16:9 landscape picture centred** (render at 1920x1080, scale to 1080x608, pad at y=656 on black). Use the full source frame; crop only to remove a logo or credit. Not a square in 9:16 ("too squished, hard to tell what's happening"), and never a raw landscape or square upload (looks wrong on TikTok).
- **Transitions:** hard cuts, short soft white flashes (about 0.1 s at 35%), and an occasional quick zoom-blur. **No pixel-mosaic or stray-coloured-block transitions**: they read as a broken video.
- **Songs (romance/non-shonen):** when the main reference's own song fits, use it; don't swap in a new one. On DUD edit 09 he rejected JVKE 'this is what falling in love feels like' over a ref built on Rihanna 'Where Have You Been (Drinks On Me remix)' ("the edit style and the music don't even hit"). The ref's dub structure and its song were made for each other. Only pick a new song when he says "music is up to you" AND no ref song suits the edit, and even then pick one whose energy matches the main ref's (EDM-orchestral drop vs. soft pop).
- **In 1:1 mode, change the font and tint but keep the ref's blend mode.** On edit 13 he called Anton with a cream-to-crimson gradient and soft shadow "looks like shit" (it read as a preset). The ref used a difference blend, so the fix kept that blend and changed only the font and tint: Dela Gothic One, `|footage - warm white|` at 94%, 0.85 x-squeeze, max width 0.72, plus a 3 px near-black outer stroke at 60% so the letters survive mid-tone footage. The renderer is `edits/13-bc-mashi-1to1/work/render.py composite_word`. Mock 4-6 options on clean frames first (`work/fontlab.py`, ~1 min) and choose from the sheet. PIL variable fonts need `set_variation_by_axes`, with the axis names mapped (Weight->wght, Width->wdth). Otherwise they silently render at the default weight.
- **Dialogue text must look designed, not captioned.** Draft1 of edit 09 showed every dub word in small white sans with a drop shadow, and he called it "completely shit, default font". The ref showed only key phrases, very big (0.2-0.3 of frame height), blended into the footage (partial opacity, behind the subject, faded vertical words) and placed in the empty part of close-ups. Mock the intro text against ref frames before the first render.
- **Lyric font in a rebuild: keep the ref's treatment, change only the face, and let him see options first.** He called a plain Anton word with a cream-to-crimson gradient fill and soft shadow "looks like shit". That swapped the ref's look (a difference blend, where the letters invert the footage) for a generic preset fill. What fixed it: copy the ref's blend (difference / screen / texture) and opacity, and change only the typeface (a heavy display face with character, e.g. Dela Gothic One) plus a small tint shift. Before rendering, mock 4-6 face + treatment options on 4 clean frames from our own timeline (`edits/13-bc-mashi-1to1/work/fontlab.py`, words off, ~20 s). Pick the best and put the option sheet in the delivery so he can choose another without a new round.
- **Dub-over-song mixes: no broadband ducking, no blinking dub beds.** On edit 09 draft2 the song ducked 7 dB under each dub line, and each dub clip brought its own anime BGM that switched on and off at the line edges. He heard it as "the music cuts off, random pauses". Keep the song level steady (the ref doesn't duck), carve space with EQ, fade line edges over 120-250 ms, and keep the intro dub bed continuous. Before hand-off, run a 20 ms RMS drop check on the mix.
- **Don't copy a ref's long holds after the drop.** He wants a new shot on every sung word / beat in the chorus ("all my life… it didn't even change scene"). Keep shots ≤0.9 s after the drop and split anything over 2 s once the song is in.
- **Never hand over a file that's still rendering.** Render to a temp name and rename it when finished; he once opened a half-written MP4 (no moov atom) and thought the whole render was broken.
- **Songs fallback:** pick by your own judgment and try new songs freely. `references/song-bank.md` is the user's list of proven romance songs, used as the fallback when unsure. Shonen: use recency data. The show's own OP/ED/insert songs are a valid lane too.
- **Deliver:** a main version, a SONG_ONLY version that lines up with the official TikTok sound (with its start offset in the notes), NO_MUSIC twins of both, a reference-vs-ours compare grid, and POSTING-NOTES.md (caption in fan voice, lowercase, emoji OK, 4-6 hashtags, exact in-app sound + start). Then the folder in Finder and a tested ZIP, per the user's standing delivery habit. Official TikTok sounds are often a fixed ~60 s clip of the song: check which song range the in-app sound covers, and when the edit's section runs past it, post the main version as original sound and say so in the notes (give the offset anyway).
- **Pick the copy level from his wording; ask only when it's unclear.**
  - "Recreate this" / "make it like this" means **1:1 but a little different**. Use the 1:1 rebuild below with its required changes (font + colour, grade, our last ~2 s, no credit card), plus 2-4 swapped filler shots.
  - "It's just completely copying" / "use refs without copying" means a **template port**: keep a proven edit's song section, cut and word timing, and signature moves, and rebuild it on a DIFFERENT anime of the SAME genre. Every shot is new, and the fonts and colours are ours.
    - It can't read as a copy, because viewers see a different show. It still hits, because the timing is locked to the song and the moves come from an edit he already loves.
    - He approved this on edit 14 (Blue Box on the edit-07 'her' template): "the edit is looking good".
    - Procedure, scripts and the rendering pitfalls: `references/template-port.md`.
  - **Templates are genre-specific.** Read what counts as a hit from the template's own numbers. The hype BC ref's hits are motion peaks on the lyric (median shot 0.77 s, no holds over 2 s). The romance DUD ref is the opposite: median shot 1.15 s, 9 holds over 2 s, about 4x less motion, and its brightest shots are 2-4 s near-still holds. Never put a hype template on romance footage.
  - The new anime brings its own versions of the template's key moments: its own couple on the literal lyrics, its own confession line in the song's pause, its own heroine on the payoff word.
- **Study what works NOW, not all-time hits.** When analysing an editor or a trend, pick posts from the last ~2 months that outperformed that account's recent median (rank by views/day; post date = TikTok id >> 32). Use at most 1-2 old hits as contrast, and keep 'works now' separate from 'used to work' in reports. The user asked for this explicitly.
- **Communicating ideas to him:** lead with the idea in 2-3 plain sentences, then at most a short table. He asked for "simple, simple terms" twice when a proposal came as a long essay. Confirm his restatement in one line before building.
- **When he wants it quick, build the payoff portion first.** Render only the ~12 s payoff section, show it, then do the full edit when he says go. The 12 s test took ~25 min, including the pickers.
- **When he asks to see a draft, show it right away**, even if QA is still running, with a 3-5 line known-issues list. Fold the QA notes and his notes into the next render together.
- **1:1 rebuild of a same-anime ref (the 'recreate' mode; overrides the 'same spirit' rule below unless he asks for an original take).** Why: free-styled "inspired by" edits kept failing his gut check (hits can't be felt from 1-4 fps sheets, the critic's taste doesn't match his, and free-styling changed the structure that made the ref work), while 1:1 copies came out good. The main ref must be from the SAME anime/character as the edit, because a ref's timing is built around its own show's moments. Copy 1:1: song, section, speed, every cut time, drops, the ref's exact source shots on the same beats (hflip when the ref is mirrored), and each word's placement/size/timing. Exception: still split any post-drop hold over ~2 s (see the long-holds rule). Rebuild from raw source footage, never from the ref's file. Only two required changes: (1) text font + colour, keeping the ref's size, placement and **blend mode** (see the font rule below), (2) the last ~2 s (a different final shot or payoff effect); always drop the ref's credit card. Optional: swap a few filler shots for equally good same-moment alternatives; revert any change that makes a key moment diverge in the ref-vs-ours grid. Side refs contribute at most 1-2 ideas in non-key spots. Never mirror/crop/speed-tweak/noise the ref's actual video to dodge TikTok detection; refuse that (it's reposting someone's work and what the unoriginal-content rules target). A source rebuild is already a new file (our grade, crops, text, clean encode); the shared official sound is fine. The bigger risk is the ref's fans calling it stolen, which the two changes handle.
- **"Like this ref, but about character X" = same spirit, not a 1:1 copy** (only when he explicitly asks for an original take; the 1:1 rebuild above is the default). Keep the main ref's song section, energy and cut rhythm, but: drop the ref editor's own spoken gags, fan call-outs ("pause if you're a ___ fan") and MADE-BY/credit cards; replace dub/English lines with the anime's own Japanese; shift the palette away from the ref's (e.g. the rival heroine's colours); write a new ending on our heroine; and borrow 3-5 concrete elements from the side refs (text treatments, insets, ending effect).
- **End on the chorus payoff, then stop.** The user cut a post-chorus tail ("after that the edit doesn't hit anymore"; Blue Box 'Strategy' now ends on "get ya get ya baby"). Don't pad past the last big chorus line; end just before the next vocal with a ~30 ms fade, and keep the final word fully in frame.
- **The user's explicit dub request wins over the Japanese-lines rule.** For Dress-Up Darling (edit 09) he asked for ENGLISH-DUB dialogue taken from Season 2 on animeclips.online (S2 there is English, S1 Japanese). When he asks for dub, use dub and keep the Japanese-line rule for edits where he doesn't say.
- **Never download a whole dub season up front (the user found it far too slow).** animeclips.online S2 is about 2,800 per-shot clips (20 GB), and its WAF bans the IP above about 3 parallel downloads (about 5 MB/s), so a full season takes about 1 hour. Instead, find the target lines first from the episode synopsis/wiki and the refs' whisper text, fetch only those 2-4 episodes, and transcribe as each one arrives.
- **Dialogue subtitles (user pref, Reze edit 11):** put the JP line + EN translation in the black bar UNDER the 16:9 picture (not over the footage), and type the EN word-by-word over the same spoken span as the JP chars so both are "live" and finish together. The engine can't draw in the bars, so render with `words: []` and composite with `edits/11-csm-reze-sad/work/subs_overlay.py` (PIL per frame, audio copied; ~1 min for 56 s).
- **Pulse and hold, not a uniform cadence, on slow score tracks.** The full rule set (1-beat cuts on the rising half of each swell, one iconic ~3.2 s hold on the falling half, black + silence before the drop, joy first with one turn, cut 2 frames early) lives in `anime-edit-thinking` → "Sad / score-music edits". Analyses: `edits/11-csm-reze-sad/analysis/analysis_{A,B}.md`. ref_dissect's on-beat % is wrong when its BPM is wrong (112 vs a true 75), so re-grid before trusting it.
- **Speed-ramped 1:1 refs (twixtor packs played 1.4-3.5x with ramps): build the picture with a frame-mapped renderer, not engine `speed`.** Pickers fit `keyframes` [ref_t, src_t] about every 0.1 s by edge-map NCC. `edits/13-bc-mashi-1to1/work/render.py` maps each output frame through those keyframes, decodes only the needed source range, grades, composites the words and pipes the result to ffmpeg (60 fps, 18.7 s in ~2.3 min). Three rules learned there: (1) hold, don't extrapolate, before a shot's first keyframe, because extrapolating backwards lands on the previous source shot; (2) frame-align the seek (`a = floor(a*fps)/fps`, `-ss a-0.0002`, t0 = a), otherwise frame 0 is ±1 frame off and the old shot flashes at cuts; (3) read the ref's word switch frames yourself (strips at -0.3..+0.05 s around each word). In edit 13 four words switched 0.07-0.15 s before the cut, which the style agent had missed.
- **Keep `in` ≥0.15 s inside the shots.json shot start.** Closer in-points showed 1-3 stray frames of the previous movie shot in the HQ render; check frames −1..+3 of every cut.
- **Brand touch that fits:** a line of the anime's own Japanese dialogue in a natural song gap, or over a smoothly ducked bed, suits the learn-Japanese brand without being a CTA. In hype edits, type the Japanese SMALL on the picture (cap about 5-6% of the picture, Noto Sans JP 800, size ~62, thin dark outline, typed per syllable, y ~0.93, off the mouth). Put the **English meaning in the black bar under the picture**, never over the footage: lowercase Montserrat 700 ~46 px, white, at canvas y ~1300, building up phrase by phrase in step with the JP. Big JP text, or EN subs on the footage, reads as clutter. Overlay helper: `edits/10-bc-refmix/work/v2/add_bar_subs.py in.mp4 out.mp4` (run it on both MAIN and NO_MUSIC).
- **Use the dialogue language the show actually has.** If he asks for a version (e.g. the English dub) that doesn't exist for a scene, use the Japanese audio and add the EN meaning in the bar, rather than stopping to ask.
- **Speed and storage matter:** the user calls multi-hour edits too slow. Target 45-75 min per edit once the anime is indexed. Never keep whole HQ seasons.

## Procedure (the approved workflow)
The approved template is `~/IkkimiAssets/edits/07-ff-her-v2/` (`work/build_spec.py`, `spec_d3.json`, `spec_song_only_d3.json`, output `draft3/`), rendered with `~/IkkimiAssets/engine/edit_engine_v2.py` (docs `engine/README_v2.md`; run with `env -u PYTHONPATH ~/IkkimiAssets/engine/.venv/bin/python`). Copy its builder for new edits rather than starting over.

0. **Verify each character's look before writing any brief** (hair colour, signature outfits) from an official character visual or the wiki, and put it in the edit's `BRIEF.md`. Model memory of anime character designs is unreliable, and a wrong description in a brief sends every subagent's shot picks and CLIP queries the wrong way.
1. **First check whether the ref was already used:** `grep -rl <tiktok_video_id> ~/IkkimiAssets/edits/*/BRIEF.md ~/IkkimiAssets/edits/*/refs` (the user re-sends refs across sessions). If a prior edit has it, reuse its dissection, aligned audio, dub clips, picks, matte cache and builder: copy the latest `build_spec_vN.py`, diff its sheet against the ref's `sheet_1fps.jpg`, and only close the gap (non-exact shots, font/colour, ending). That turns a ~70 min edit into ~40 min.
   **Dissect the references with the deterministic tool, not by hand:** `env -u PYTHONPATH ~/IkkimiAssets/engine/.venv/bin/python ~/IkkimiAssets/pipeline/ref_dissect.py <out> <url_or_mp4>[::<song file or YouTube URL>] ...`. It takes about 10-20 s for 5 refs and writes `SUMMARY.md` plus per-ref `report.json` (cuts, beats, song offset/speed/splice segments, LUFS, aspect box) and `sheet_1fps` / `cuts_strip` / `hook_4fps` jpgs. Read the summary, and look only at the images you need for style (text fonts, sizes, layering, effects). Also run `whisper-cli -m ~/.hermes/cache/whisper/ggml-small.bin -l auto -f ref.wav` on the main ref: it shows the dub lines, editor voice-overs and gags (and their times) in seconds, which the cut list alone hides. Count unique ref ids in the summary: two short links can resolve to the same post. Without a song, its BPM can be a few BPM off (112 vs a true 110), so re-run with `::<song master>` before building a beat grid. A weak or no-match song offset means the wrong song version: Shazam it, because it may be a cover. Then run a mel-corr speed scan at 0.80-1.06 against the master. Hype refs often use a slowed copy (0.82-0.85x), and two refs on the 'same' sound can differ. Tell the user which speed each ref uses and pick one on purpose, because retiming later means rebuilding every cut. Docs: `~/IkkimiAssets/pipeline/README.md`.
2. **Index the footage lean** (run with `~/IkkimiAssets/pipeline/.venv-clip/bin/python`): `pipeline/aniclips_lean.py find <name>`, then `index <slug> ~/IkkimiAssets/footage/lean` (preview-only, about 30 s per episode), then `clip_index.py build` and `clip_index.py op-ed`. For footage not on aniclips, use `index-local` on a folder of official clips or the user's files. Blue Box and Black Clover are NOT on aniclips (114-title catalog), and Black Clover isn't on animeclips.online either, but Dress-Up Darling S1 and S2 are on both. When he links these sites as "sources" for a show they don't carry, say so and use the fresh-episode twixtor-pack route in `anime-footage-scene-index` (16 packs fetched + indexed in under 2 min). Blue Box already has a local index: `footage/lean/bb_official/` (official OP/ED/PV/Netflix JP clips plus flagged fan scenepacks from `footage/blue-box/`; `sources.json` and `static_overlays.json` list burned-text boxes). Details: `references/aniclips-source.md`.
3. **Story first, then plan.** Load `anime-edit-thinking` and write the storyboard (3-5 beats with a turn) and the ref likes in BRIEF.md before any slot plan. Then plan (one agent): the song section (one continuous section whose energy curve fits the storyboard, starting at the phrase start, never mid-phrase); every sung word timed to the frame (whisper word stamps snapped to vocal onsets and cross-checked against the reference's on-screen times; lrclib line times when whisper can't hear a scream); per-word typography measured from the reference frames (font, scale_x squeeze, size, colour, position, layering, behind-subject); and shot slots on the reference's cut times with a literal-lyric wish per slot.
4. **Pick shots:** per slot, `clip_index.py search --image f20 --image f50 --image f80 --exclude_op`, which uses 3 ref frames at 20/50/80% of the ref shot averaged together and so suppresses kinetic text. On FF this found the exact shot in the top 5 for 13 of 15 refs. Text queries also work, at about 80% precision@8. Then verify the hits by eye against the reference frame in one contact sheet. **Quality guard** (the user won't accept a speed-up that lowers edit quality): CLIP only shortlists and never picks. Review the **top 20** (exact-shot recall was 13/15 at top 5 but 15/15 at top 20). If nothing in the top 20 matches the reference well, search deeper (text queries, neighbouring shots of the same scene, the LLM index where one exists) before settling. Fetch HQ only for the chosen clips (`fetch-hq`). Index with `--no-keep-preview` (about 150 MB per show) unless previews are needed for picking motion. Depth and pitfalls: `references/footage-matching.md`.
5. **Draft fast, then one independent QA subagent, then show.** The builder goes spec → full MAIN render (about 85 s on a cached matte, ~4 min with fresh mattes on the PC) → decode check + a 1-3 fps sheet glance. Then, before the user sees anything, spawn ONE QA subagent using the brief in `anime-edit-thinking/templates/qa-brief.md` (harsh-editor review of the hook, audio, word timing, payoff shot, text and every cut). Fix what it flags and show the user (the user's standing rule; a draft shown without it came back with a weak opening, bad audio and the wrong payoff shot). After he has seen it, one fix agent folds in his notes via `USER-NOTES.md` and renders the SONG_ONLY + NO_MUSIC twins. Technical QA: full decode check, ebur128 at about -10 LUFS / true peak ≤ -1 dBTP, and frame-accurate grabs at every changed window.
6. **Show the user** the video plus the sheet, ask what feels off, and apply his notes. His notes outrank any critic. Assemble the full delivery folder (step 'Deliver') from the fixed draft.
7. **Clean up:** delete HQ clips not used by the edit or the best-shot lists, and keep the indexes and thumbnails (any clip can be re-fetched in seconds).

## The reference style he approved (kinetic-lyric romance)
Every sung word on screen the frame it's sung, with a hard on. A giant condensed display serif keyword (60-95% of frame height, squeezed horizontally, letter jitter, dark outline + hard offset shadow) layered with a small script connector word on top. Palette: red #E23A4A, blush pink, cream, lavender, white. About 30% of shots put the big word behind the character (rembg matte). Occasional white-outlined sticker cut-outs on cream. Falling sakura petals and white specks, warm light leaks, bloom, a bright soft-vivid grade, and a pink-rim night grade. Shots follow the lyric literally. The ending holds on the heroine with a font-cycling payoff word and an oval inset flickering through memory shots. Reference dissections: `~/IkkimiAssets/edits/remake-ref{1..5}/analysis/style.md`.

## Orchestration
- **Fan-out that works for a new show:** write `edits/<NN-name>/BRIEF.md` (request, characters, main ref, paths) and have every agent read it. Then run 3 agents in parallel: (1) footage sourcing + `index-local` + a verified heroine shortlist + clean Japanese dialogue candidates; (2) song master + offset + word timing + typography + slot plan + spliced audio with a beat grid; (3) side-ref style-borrow notes + fan-favourite scenes. Next, 2 pickers split by slot range write `picks/picks_A.json` / `picks_B.json`, check each other's file to avoid duplicates. Finally one builder renders.
- **Timing to expect** (new show, nothing indexed): parallel stage about 30 min (footage sourcing is the long pole), pickers about 16 min, builder to draft1 about 13 min, fix pass about 6 min: roughly 65-75 min to a postable draft. Give the user these numbers when he asks for an ETA.
- **In a 1:1 rebuild, use the ref's own audio track** (user's call on edit 09: "strip off the original audio, problem solved"). Extract it from the ref MP4 (`-vn -ar 44100`), keep it on the ref timeline, and splice only our own ending: crossfade 40 ms into our tail, level-matched (the ref ran ~9 dB under our -10 LUFS mix), then +7.5 dB into `alimiter` to about -12 LUFS / -1.4 dBFS. Remux onto the picture with `-c:v copy`. Rebuilding the dub from episode snippets lost to the ref every time: the ref's intro dub is slowed about 0.87x and gated, so 1x snippets never sat right, and the user kept hearing lines cut off. The ref's own stutters (DUD ref's 'If, if') come with it.
- **Subagents run on Sonnet 5.5** (`delegation.model claude-sonnet-5-5`, set at the user's request for speed). Split audio from picture: one agent builds the mix in `work/audio_vN/` on the fixed ref timeline while the builder renders with the old mix, then remux the new WAV in seconds (`ffmpeg -i v.mp4 -i mix.wav -map 0:v -map 1:a -c:v copy -c:a aac -b:a 320k`), so no re-render is needed.
- **Dub dialogue must never be cut off** (user complaint on edit 09 draft2: Marin's and Gojo's lines 'keep getting cut off'). Lay each line as one continuous episode span found by xcorr against the ref's own dialogue, run it ≥250 ms past the last word plus a 120-200 ms fade, and never trim a snippet at a word end. Check with whisper word-level (every word once, no stutter) and a 20 ms RMS scan (no >20 dB drop within 20 ms).
- **More agents don't speed up the build stage:** spec → render → check is one serial chain on shared files, so give it to a single builder. Extra parallel agents pay off at sourcing/planning, picking (split by slot range) and post-feedback work (SONG_ONLY, twins, grid, notes, fixes to separate sections).
- Run at most about 4 heavy agents at once (render, rembg, vision). More pushed the Mac load past 60 and doubled render times. On the Windows PC the cap is 3 CLIP-searching pickers and nothing overlapping a render (see `ikkimi-windows-pc`). Each `clip_index.py search` call reloads SigLIP, so 5 parallel pickers timed out their searches and froze his PC. Standing preference: use as many subagents as speed needs, but cut the count when the PC is already busy (check `nvidia-smi` GPU % first, since he may be gaming) and have each child wrap heavy commands in `scripts/run_low.py --cpus 4`.
- **Time-box analysis subagents to ~10-15 min** and put the deadline in the brief ("write the report by T+12 min with what you have"). An open-ended "analyse deeply" pair ran 28+ min and the user asked why it was taking so long.
- The bottleneck is agent reasoning and vision, not the CPU. Rendering takes 3-5 min per version. Don't recommend moving to a faster PC.
- Keep mechanical work in scripts (`pipeline/`). Spend agent tokens on taste only: style reading, shot verification and comparison.
- Only one agent at a time may use the logged-in TikTok scraper profile.

## Pitfalls
- **Text placement:** keep giant words off faces unless they're deliberately behind the subject. Measure each word's box against the reference frame and use the subject matte bbox. A width clamp that recentres words pushes them onto faces.
- **Behind-subject words vanish** when the matte fills the frame or a raised arm covers them, so reposition or rescale them, or put them on top.
- **Size text for a phone, not the 1920 canvas:** the 16:9 picture is only 1080 px wide on screen, so a subtitle that looks fine in a 1920 still is unreadable. Lyric words: cap 15-22% of the picture for connector words and 30-50% for keywords (in a rebuild, the ref's cap height wins). A plain fill in a stock condensed face (Anton, Bebas) reads as a default font, so give it a real treatment (see the font rule). Typed Japanese lines in hype edits stay small (see Brand touch). Thin cream script on bright shots needs a dark outline/shadow. In font-cycle endings, check every font in the cycle on its own frame: a wider face can clip the frame edge while the others fit.
- **Never cover the payoff shot with the payoff word.** A giant centred slam word over the hero's entrance hides the very moment the edit builds to. On payoff shots, put the word small in the bottom or top band (height ~0.2, y 0.83) and keep the subject clear.
- **Grade:** a full-frame vignette turns white sticker and high-key backgrounds grey, so keep it corner-only and off on high-key shots. Per-clip grades must actually override the global look (check the merge).
- **zsh doesn't word-split** a command stored in a variable. Put multi-render runs in a bash script.
- Hermes `local=true` browser is headless, so the user can't see its tabs or solve CAPTCHAs there. For logins, open a visible Playwright window.
- Leftover tabs in the hidden automation browser can autoplay audio the user hears but can't find. Close audio-capable tabs (TikTok, YouTube, games) via CDP `Target.closeTarget` after browsing. Research subagents don't know this rule, so put it in every research child's context. A child once left a @duolingo TikTok tab playing for 27 minutes. To find the culprit on the Mac, run `pmset -g assertions | grep 'Playing audio'` for the Chrome PID (on Windows, check the Chrome/Edge tab audio indicator or list the tabs directly). Then list the tabs with `curl localhost:$(head -1 ~/.hermes/browser-profile/chrome/DevToolsActivePort)/json`, and close the tiktok, youtube and shazam tabs with `/json/close/<id>`.
- **Dub over a song: never duck the song broadband, and never let a line's anime bed blink on/off.** On DUD 09 draft2 the user heard a 7 dB duck plus beds starting and stopping at line edges as "the music cuts off, random pauses". Fix (`edits/09-dud-dub-edit/work/audio_v3/build_v3.py`): no duck (at most a -2 dB 1-4 kHz dip on the song under the line), +3 dB 2-4 kHz on the dub, 200 ms equal-power line fades, the monologue played as one continuous source with crossfades at speech-free points, and its own dips filled with looped room tone. Verify with `drop_check.py` (20 ms RMS vs ±1 s median, >10 dB).
- **After the drop, cut on every sung word and on every beat a word is held (shots ≤0.9 s); don't copy a ref's 3+ s holds there.** The user said a 3.6 s hold on 'all my life' "didn't even change scene". Re-enter the giant word on each cut, matte-checked per shot (`addsplit` in `edits/09-dud-dub-edit/work/build_spec_v3.py`).
