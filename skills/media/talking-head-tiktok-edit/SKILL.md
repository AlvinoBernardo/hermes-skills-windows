## Scripts (before the shoot)
The user often drafts a spoken script for a talking-head clip and asks to "improve" it.
- Keep his own wording and voice ("I just heard this online…", "dude", "peace"). Make light edits: tighten the hook, use short one-breath sentences, keep the contrast words consistent (dumb vs smart), and end on a payoff line. List what changed in 2-4 bullets.
- Don't use Noxy, his app or his founder story as the example unless he asks. Use universal examples (math, money, fitness, famous stories) instead. Mark anecdotes that are probably embellished as "there's a famous story…".
- Point out grammar slips ("dumb people is") but don't force the fix. Captions later follow what he actually said.
- When he asks "do you agree?", give an honest answer with nuance. A caveat line usually makes a good closing beat that drives comments.
- Give the length in seconds, one optional extra beat (an example to insert), and an on-screen title suggestion.
- Never add lines he wouldn't say, such as jokes about named friends or invented reactions. When he strikes a line, delete it rather than rewording it, and keep his "how I found this" backstory beat instead of jumping straight to the point. Full rules are in yap-video-format step 3.
- When he wants a NEW talking video planned from scratch (topic only, no draft), use `yap-video-format`. It has the data-backed hook, beat and ending templates. This skill is for the edit once the footage exists.
- **Lesson / law series scripts (e.g. one video per law of a book).** He wants a word-for-word script in HIS three-beat shape, not a book summary and not an Aiden-style kit:
  1. **Rule + stakes:** "You should never [rule], because if you do, [consequence]."
  2. **Story:** "A great example of this is a guy named [real person]…": one famous true story, told in plain everyday words ("the money guy for the King"), with 1-2 vivid details and the punchy turn ("Everybody loved it, except the king").
  3. **Takeaway:** "So the key takeaway is…". Build it around HIS own line when he gives one, and keep his wording.
  - Add a bridge line between story and takeaway that names the real lesson of the story ("His mistake wasn't being too good. It was trying to shine too early, in front of the wrong person"). Without it the story says one thing and the takeaway another, and he'll say the message isn't clear.
  - Don't open with the series tag ("Law 1 of 48…") or with a modern hypothetical ("your boss in a meeting"). The opener is the rule itself, and the example is a historical or famous story. The series line goes at the end, right before "Peace".
  - Fact-check the story, and soften his dramatic words when they're false ("you will get killed" → "you could lose everything"). Tell him in one line why.
  - Keep the explanation in the reply short: the script, then 1-2 notes. On a revision, reprint the changed beat; when he says "full script", reprint all of it with every accepted change. Series rules (layout, takeaway bar, approved takeaways) live in yap-video-format `references/explainer-series-voice.md`.

## Pipeline (worked on M5 Mac, ~2 min total for 60s clip)
1. **Probe**: iPhone .MOV = HEVC 10-bit HLG (arib-std-b67) Dolby Vision, 4K, rotation -90. Homebrew ffmpeg has NO zscale/libplacebo/drawtext/subtitles → don't fight it.
2. **HDR→SDR + rotate** (Windows: `scripts/hdr2sdr.py`, see above): `avconvert -s IN.MOV -p Preset1920x1080 -o sdr1080.mp4 --replace` (macOS built-in, ~9s, proper tonemap, outputs 1080x1920 bt709). This is the fix for "greyed/washed" HLG look.
3. **Grade** on top (approved look): `eq=contrast=1.10:saturation=1.22:gamma=0.97,colorbalance=rm=-0.02:bm=0.03:bs=-0.02,unsharp=5:5:0.5`.
4. **Transcribe** (Windows: `scripts/wtranscribe.py`, see above): uv venv py3.11 + `mlx-whisper`, model `mlx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo`, `word_timestamps=True`. Pass `initial_prompt` with jargon only ("vibe coding, ADE, Orca Dev"), otherwise it hears "vibe going" or "switch up". Never put full script sentences in the prompt: Whisper then skips the matching opening words. Re-transcribe the cut with no prompt.
5. **Pick segments**: drop false starts/retakes (usually first ~15-20s), long pauses, fillers (dude/okay/now/basically/so-yeah outro). Use silencedetect + 40ms RMS envelope to place cuts in dips. Segments via trim/atrim + 20-30ms afade at each edge, concat.
6. **Cuts (user feedback)**: user HATES many cuts + random punch-in zooms. Keep long continuous takes — only cut out dead air/retakes BETWEEN big sections (aim ≤4 segments per minute), NO zoom alternation (ZOOM all 1.0). Mid-sentence fillers like "dude" stay in the audio; just drop them from captions.
6a. **"No pause time, I'm constantly talking"** (IMG_1417): the user then wants EVERY pause inside sentences cut too, which overrides the "few cuts" rule. Find silences ≥0.2s with a 10ms RMS envelope below about -34 dB, including held words like "so…" and "to… the" and the gaps before "Think about it" and "Peace". Cut each one to about 0.05-0.1s and drop quiet fillers ("dude"). Verify by re-transcribing the cut: there should be no word gaps over 0.2s and no lost words. A 0.19s single-word segment is fine. The music stem falls about 3 dB (the voice ducks it more often), so raise the vol to about 1.95 to get back to -22. 30s raw → 15.4s. **That was TOO aggressive: the user said "a lot of my words are getting cut off".** Cutting 0.01-0.02s before the RMS onset, with a 0.03s afade, eats the attack of th/p/m/s words, and cutting a held "so…" right after its burst clips the vowel. The approved v3 (16.3s) keeps about 0.08s before every onset and about 0.10s after every tail (the RMS floor, about -40 dB), and lets a held vowel decay for about 0.15s. Its afades are 0.02 in / 0.03 out. Before rendering, print the 30ms RMS max at every cut point: each in- and out-level should be ≤ about -35 dB.
6b. **Endings**: drop a line he abandons halfway (trails off, then silence). Keep the closing "Peace" with a "peace" caption, even if he waves at the camera while saying it. Drop it only when the lens is actually covered, and check a frame to confirm. End about 0.15s after the word with a 0.2s video fade.
6c. **Reshoots** ("use this video instead"): copy the previous clip's cut.py/render.py/mix.sh into a new scratch dir. Remap the SEG list, the PLAN captions and the card trigger times to the new words. Keep the same title, card, colours and music settings so the look stays consistent. Every re-cut shifts Whisper word starts by up to ~0.15s, so widen the PLAN-vs-words assert tolerance (≈0.15) rather than re-timing everything.
6d. **No-stutter gate (every edit, especially when he says "no stutter").** Restarts hide in Whisper's full pass, so do all of these:
   - BEFORE picking segments: run `scripts/snip.py` over the RAW file in 3.5s windows (2.5s step). It exposes the restarts ("I just heard this / I just heard this on…", "you would think that the fl— / you would think that the fly") that the full pass merges into one stretched word.
   - Place every join with `scripts/dips.py SRC -38 a-b …` (lists real pauses with their min dB; `raw` mode prints the 10 ms values to find onsets/tails). A short stutter word ("for, for") can be removed with a ≤0.3 s micro-cut only when both edges sit in dips ≤ -44 dB.
   - Test every planned join with `scripts/tcat.py FILE:a:b+FILE:c:d` before rendering: it must read as one clean sentence with no missing or doubled word. If tcat prints a doubled word at a join ("yes and and guess"), the retake itself starts with a restart ("And, — and guess what"): tcat the take's first 0.5 s alone and start after the restart's dip.
   - AFTER the cut: scan the cut transcript for adjacent duplicate words and for any word stretched >0.5s, snip the whole cut in 3.5s windows, and snip the first 0.6s of the hook on its own.
   - Finally transcribe the delivered MAIN mp4 with `condition_on_previous_text=False`. It can catch words the cut pass dropped (a quick "dude" between sentences).
   - A stretched word is not always a stutter: if the RMS in the gap stays clearly above the noise floor (e.g. -37 vs -50 dB) he is vocalising an emphasis ("so… much"), so keep it. A real pause of ≤0.3s mid-sentence also stays: removing it needs a micro jump cut, and a 5-frame skip of his head reads as a VISUAL stutter, which is worse.
   - Visual stutter check: "no stutter" also means the picture. Confirm the SDR source has a constant frame interval (`ffprobe -select_streams v -show_entries packet=pts_time` → all deltas ≈1/30), force `fps=30` in cut.py, and put every cut between whole sentences.
7. **Re-transcribe the cut** to get final word times → captions: break at punctuation or 3 words (2 if long). UPPERCASE Avenir Next Heavy (`/System/Library/Fonts/Avenir Next.ttc` index 8), 92px, 9px black stroke, soft shadow, keyword highlight yellow (255,196,0), pop-in scale 0.82→1 over 0.12s, y≈1330 (chest, below face). Auto-shrink to max 940px width.
8. **Overlays rendered with PIL** as RGBA frames piped to ffmpeg rawvideo stdin, overlaid on the cut. Orange pill headers ("TIP #1 · …") at y=250.
9. **B-roll** when speaker says "it looks like this": full-frame blurred-darkened bg + large rounded card (1000x1180) slow-panning across a product screenshot (Orca: github.com/stablyai/orca docs/assets/readme-hero.jpg), label above, captions moved to y=1640.
10. **Audio**: voice highpass 80 + acompressor + loudnorm I=-14. Music must be clearly audible (user said too quiet at vol 0.16-0.22 + ratio 5-6 duck): use **vol ≈0.42 (music ≈11 dB under voice), gentle duck threshold 0.05 ratio 2.5 attack 20 release 350**, `-stream_loop -1` if track shorter than video, fade in/out, alimiter. Final ≈ -14 LUFS.
11. **Proof overlays where he points**: when the speaker points at empty space while saying a number ("my first $120"), find the pointing frames (contact sheet at 4fps), locate fingertip, and pop a rounded screenshot card (500px wide, shadow, 0.14s scale-in) with its bottom just above the fingertip; hide the title while it's up. Revenue screenshot: `~/PersonalBrandAssets/broll/screenshots/revenue_120_last_28_days.png`.

Reference implementation from first run: scratch dir `~/.hermes/cache/scratch/vid/` (cut2.py, render.py) — may be pruned; rebuild from the steps above.

## Pitfalls
- Windows: see "On Windows" at the top (venv, whisper-cli, fonts); the Mac notes below still describe the Mac setup.
- Env: the mlx-whisper venv is `~/.hermes/cache/scratch/wv` (symlink it as .venv). PIL is in system `python3`, not in that venv.
- Hermes browser may fail if Chrome holds real-profile lock → use curl/curl_cffi instead.
- web_extract backend may be search-only → curl directly.
- Terminal foreground >600s gets backgrounded; keep renders single-pass (~80s for 27s video).
- Chain the steps as `python3 render.py && bash mix.sh && cp … ~/Downloads/`, never with `;`. Otherwise a render that fails its assert still mixes and copies the STALE overlay, and the user gets the old video under the new name.
- Scratch venvs (`wv`, `ytv`) get pruned after about 24h of idle. Recreate them with `uv venv wv -p 3.11 && uv pip install -p wv mlx-whisper` (and the same for `ytv` with yt-dlp) before transcribing.
