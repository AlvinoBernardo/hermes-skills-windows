# Voice and humor: how he "explains shit"

## The voice in one paragraph
He sounds like a smart, slightly unhinged CS kid explaining something to his group chat. He's fast and over-specific, with zero pauses, and he never sounds like a teacher. He stacks real numbers, then undercuts every flex with a dry joke. He swears casually, uses internet slang, and talks about himself with ironic grandiosity ("my audience of 5,000 followers").

## Techniques (with lines from the hits)
1. **The deflating one-liner right after a big fact.** This is his signature move.
   - "He qualified for the AIME five times. Yes, five is a larger number than two."
   - "So he's one of the kids that cured cancer before graduating high school."
   - "I thought I'd find some crazy juice, but nope, it's a regular app."
2. **Absurdly specific comparisons.**
   - "creating Azerbaijan shrimp futures for a 0.001% edge"
   - "people on this app have the attention span of a ham sandwich"
   - "all the fancy cool stuff happens on some cluster in Bezos' basement"
3. **Ironic self-roast after a flex.**
   - "I'm starting to think we are low-key the sole reason AI uses as much water as it does."
   - "I'm stingy and don't want to pay for credits, so I have Codex do code review."
   - "Your first business is never about making money. It's about getting good. At least that's how I'm coping."
4. **Insider jargon, explained in half a sentence.** Viewers feel smart, and it signals authority. Jargon explained this way had a 1.31x median.
   - "Zetamac, the mental math game quants use"
   - "Goldwater Scholar — the most prestigious undergrad STEM award in existence"
5. **Pre-empting the comment section.**
   - "Now I know what you're thinking…"
   - "And before you say anything like, that's unethical — welcome to the game, my friend."
6. **Fake-formal closers and anti-CTAs.**
   - "In summary, Neel mogs Roy in both intelligence and height."
   - "Comment real on this video and nothing will happen because I am too cheap for ManyChat."
   - "Hashtag agentic or something. Bye."
   - "My Claude didn't cook up the banger in time, so this post is a slop one. My bad."
7. **Analogies for anything abstract.**
   - AI with no authority is like a store manager who can't pay a plumber.
   - Idle AI subscriptions are "like a gym membership you never use".
   - "These dudes collect offers like infinity stones while we're here becoming statistics."
8. **Direct "you" scenarios.**
   - "Have you ever scrolled on Hinge, screenshotted a really hot girl, and sent it to your boys? Hinge knows."
   - "Picture this. It's 1am. You type something that might get you banned…"

## Delivery
- **Say the hook in one breath.** Don't add a greeting or a "so" before it. His first word lands at 0.0 s.
- **Throw the jokes away.** Same speed and a straight face. Don't pause for a laugh.
- **Point at the receipts** (whiteboard, the area where a card will pop in) as you name them.

## Adapting to this user
- Keep the user's own voice ("I just heard this online…", "dude", "Peace") and his habit of cutting the "hope it helps" outro.
- Borrow the techniques, not Aiden's identity jokes. Swap in identity angles the user actually owns (founder of a calorie app, vibe coder, his own real stats). Ask rather than guess.
- Every joke slot in a plan should offer a suggested line, but mark it optional. He improvises.
