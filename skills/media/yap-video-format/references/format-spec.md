# Format spec: the look and sound of an Aiden-style yap reel

Taken from the frames of his hits and his own description of the edit ("how I edit reels with Claude Code", 300k views, 26.85x). Source reels are 720x1280; the positions below are scaled to 1080x1920.

## Set and talent
- **One fixed spot:** his dorm, standing in front of the bed with 2-3 big posters behind (Korean soju ads, a "Texas Fight" banner). There's a whiteboard in the room for chart and stat videos. The setting stays the same across the whole series, which builds recognition.
- **Framing:** a vertical selfie or tripod shot, waist up or knees up, standing. He's centred and the head sits in the top third, below the title box.
- **Mic:** a small wireless lav held in his hand like a handheld mic, or clipped on. Holding it is a visual cue that says "I'm about to tell you something".
- **Wardrobe:** a plain or graphic tee. He wears the same "Trogdor the Burninator" shirt over and over; he jokes that it's his lucky shirt, and it's become a recognisable signature. The lesson is to have one recurring signature item.
- **Energy:** hands constantly moving and pointing at things, with a deadpan face on the jokes. Early July videos were a tight, moody close-up selfie in a gaming chair, and those worked too (34x). The constant is a face filling the frame and talking straight to the lens.

## On-screen text
- **Title box (all the hits have one):** a white rounded rectangle with black, medium-weight text in sentence case or lowercase, in TikTok's native "text with background" look. It sits at the top (y ≈ 230-330 on 1920), is centred, uses 1-2 lines and stays on screen the whole video. It's either a punchier restatement of the hook ("I reverse engineered Hinge") or a POV/identity line ("POV: the CS job market is cooked", "If you're watching this video / You're not gonna make it in quant").
- **Captions:** 2-3 words at a time in ALL CAPS, white with a thin black outline, in a semi-bold sans (Montserrat/TikTok Sans-like). They sit mid-frame at chest/waist (y ≈ 1050-1300) and use hard swaps with no animation. Early videos used mixed-case white captions without the outline. Numbers stay numerals ("$3 MILLION/YEAR SALARY").
- **Numbered section labels** on list videos ("NUMBER 3, BABY-PROOF", "THING TWO") appear as captions or a small tag, and they're what makes the list feel savable.
- **For this user's edits:** the title box maps to the persistent 2-line title (style B in talking-head-tiktok-edit, or a white-pill variant). Captions follow his existing style A or B; don't force a change of caption style unless he asks for "the Aiden look".

## "Receipts" every ~3 seconds (the real retention engine)
There's a pop-up roughly every 3 s, landing on the word that names it. Each one is a rounded-corner card, about 60-90% of the frame width, in the upper-middle of the frame over the body. The face stays visible.
- the token-usage chart ("6.78B") on "I burned 6 billion"
- app logos on brand names (Hinge, Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini…)
- a screenshot of the GitHub repo, tweet, TechCrunch headline or IG profile card of the person he's talking about
- his real terminal output, prompts and agent reports, with a coloured label tab ("my prompt, last night", "morning report")
- the whiteboard, with a hand-drawn chart or stat list he points at
- meme images and reaction stills on the joke beats
- a collage of his own day-N thumbnails when he talks about his content

**Hits used real artifacts, not stock footage.** In the user's pipeline this is the card system in talking-head-tiktok-edit: screenshot cards, profile cards, counters and checklists.

## Pacing and audio
- **Delivery:** about 240-280 wpm with no pauses over 0.6 s and the first word at 0.0 s. The rough cut removes every breath, filler and retake (WhisperX word timestamps). Fast talking alone didn't separate hits from flops, though: hits and flops had the same wpm (241 vs 239).
- **Cuts:** hard cuts, with "a zoom every once in a while". His edits ran at about 1.5 scene changes per 10 s on hits, which is fewer than on his flops (1.8). For this user: no punch-in zooms; he dislikes them.
- **Music:** a quiet bed under the voice. He chooses by mood: half trending audio, half "Plants vs. Zombies and anime soundtracks". Use the music rules in talking-head-tiktok-edit, with the user's library first.
- **Length:** 60-120 s for list and story videos, and 20-40 s for a stat + twist bit.

## Ending
- **Content ending:** an anticlimax ("I thought I'd find some crazy juice, but nope, it's a regular app"), a twist ("Oh wait, this is the dating chart. Oops."), or an absurd CTA ("Comment real… nothing will happen because I am too cheap for ManyChat").
- **Series sign-off,** said fast: "This is day 84 of posting every day until my dating app has 3,000 daily active users. Follow along for the journey. Peace."
- **Bridge line before the sign-off:** "If any of this stuff about X interests you, …". It ties the topic back to why you'd follow.
- **For this user:** jump from the last content line straight to "Peace" (his preference). Use the series line only if he's running a series.
