# The user's own explainer-series voice (book lessons, "48 Laws of Power" series)

Use this for any "one concept per video" series where the user teaches an idea from a book (48 Laws of Power, one law per video). It REPLACES the Aiden kit for these videos. Don't add a title box, joke slots, receipts, alternate hooks or a talking-points layout unless he asks for them.

## Procedure
1. **Get the law right.** Know the law in plain words, plus both halves when it has two (Law 2: don't over-trust friends AND learn to use enemies). Make the hook line obviously the book's law. Law 3's "Conceal Your Intentions" became "never tell people what you're really planning". If he asks "are you sure that's what the law means?", quote the book's one-line summary and map each beat to it in 3 bullets, no more.
2. **Pick the example: a famous real person, never the book's own story.** The viewer should already know the name (Steve Jobs, Caesar, Zuckerberg, a famous athlete or founder). The book's examples read as "just reading the book". The best stories prove both halves of the law in one arc. Law 2 used Jobs: his friend Sculley got him kicked out, then his rival Bill Gates put $150M into Apple.
3. **Write it in his shape** (below), in words a 14-year-old gets on the first listen.
4. **Fact-check every date, number and outcome** before delivering (see Accuracy).
5. **Deliver it in the teleprompter layout** (below), with one short music line and a one-line fact check under the script.
6. **On revisions, reprint only the changed section** (usually TAKEAWAY + OUTRO) in the same layout, and say that the rest stays the same. When he says "full script" (usually once a section is settled), reprint all four beats with every accepted change, unchanged beats word for word, plus the length/music/fact-check lines. Use his replacement wording as he said it. Cut the lines he cuts; don't reword them. If he goes back to an earlier version ("I like yours better, revert it"), restore it exactly.
7. **When the hook changes, re-check that the story still proves it, and say so plainly.** A new hook can shift the point. "Better hated than ignored" needs a backlash beat (people attacked him, and he won anyway), which a story about pure amazement doesn't have. Give the honest yes/partly/no first, then either a minimal fix (add 2-3 true backlash lines and reprint only the STORY) or a better-fitting example, and also adjust the TAKEAWAY when the old one argued a different half of the law.

## The shape (his words: "you should never ___, because if you do ___. A great example of this is a guy that ___")
1. **HOOK, the rule + the stakes:** "You should never [the law in plain words]. Because if you do… [dramatic consequence]." He may instead want the book's own key line as the hook (Law 6: "It is better to be attacked, even slandered, than ignored"). Find the exact line in the book's text before printing it, and offer one plain-word version for any hard word ("slandered" became "hated on" on camera).
2. **STORY:** "A great example of this is a guy named [X]…", retold like he's telling a friend:
   - short sentences and casual words, with no titles or jargon ("chamberlain", "Byzantine", "co-emperor")
   - one inner-thought line (*"This is my guy. He's got my back."*). **He delivers italic thoughts on camera as real quotes ("and he literally said this").** So write it as a feeling (*"This is my guy."*), or use a line the person verifiably said. Never write a punchy quotable line that the person didn't actually say.
   - time jumps as their own lines ("Two years later…", "Twelve years later…")
   - a concrete fall, then a twist, then a one-line mirror ("His friend kicked him out… and his enemy helped save the company.")
3. **TAKEAWAY:** "So the key takeaway is: …". It needs a sharp, strategic, long-game power angle, which is the reason people watch 48 Laws content. Explain *why* the law works, then give the move, and stay close to what the book actually advises. He may bring a book line he likes. Try it, but he may still prefer your version.
   - **Make it easy to follow:** open with the law as a plain move ("let other people do the work… but make sure your name is on it"), then one reason line, then a closer. If the story beat has side twists (a comeback, an inner-thought line), cut them so the story proves ONE point that the takeaway repeats.
   - **Don't restate the hook's payoff** in the takeaway's last lines (hook "helpers get forgotten, you get remembered" + closer "nobody remembers who did the work" = said twice). Use the closer for the long-game reason (money, power, the next opportunity) or a book quote that loops back to the hook.
   - **Book quotes he asks for loosely** ("life is too short blah blah") may come from the book's PREFACE, not the law's chapter. Search the book text for the exact line ("In the end, life is short, opportunities are few, and you have only so much energy to draw on"), shorten it for speech, and say where it's from.
4. **OUTRO:** "That's the [Nth] law of 48 Laws of Power. Peace."

The target is 40-60 s read aloud (Law 1's final cut was 52.5 s).

## Teleprompter layout (mandatory)
He found a block of prose "very very hard to read" while filming. Format it like this:
- Bold emoji labels for each beat: **🎬 HOOK**, **📖 STORY**, **🔑 TAKEAWAY**, **👋 OUTRO**, with `---` between beats.
- One short sentence per line and a blank line between lines. Use "…" where he should pause.
- Bold the names, numbers and punch words (**Steve Jobs**, **150 million dollars**, **Call your enemies.**).
- Inner thoughts go in *italics*. Time jumps ("**Two years later…**") get their own bold line.

## Approved takeaways (the bar to hit)
- **Law 1:** "Don't try to outshine your master. Play it smart and make them look good, because a master will fall someday. If you play it right, you'll outlive him, and someday you'll outshine him."
- **Law 2:** "Friends get comfortable. They start thinking they deserve what you gave them. An enemy has everything to prove. So keep your friends as friends… but never make them your work partners. When it comes to something important… don't call your friends. Call your enemies."
  - The middle line is the book's "keep friends for friendship, not for work" idea. He didn't want "keep your friends close" or "never give them the keys".
  - He also rejected a takeaway that opens with a quote ("your closest friend could become your greatest enemy").
- **Law 7** (hook = the book's judgment line "You should never do yourself what others can do for you… because in the end the people who helped you get forgotten, and you get remembered"): "You can't do everything yourself. So let other people do the work… but make sure your name is on it. Because whoever's name is on it gets the money, the power, and the next opportunity. And remember… 'Life is short, opportunities are few, and you only have so much energy.' So don't waste it doing what others can do for you." A quote is fine as the CLOSER (it echoes the hook); it's opening with one that he rejected.

## What he rejected (don't repeat these)
- **The book's own historical example** (for Law 2, Michael III / Basil): "don't take the example from the book".
- **Hard-to-follow wording:** obscure history, titles and long sentences. Make it easy to understand.
- **An Aiden-style kit** (cold-open hook, series line up front, a 🃏 joke): it isn't his style for this series.
- **"Law number one: … Basically that means …" followed by a modern hypothetical** (correcting your boss in a meeting): he said it sounds like reading the book. The example has to be a story about a real person.
- **A soft, moral takeaway** ("be good, make your boss look good and they'll keep you around"): he called it shit. It needs the cunning, long-game angle.

## Accuracy
- Keep his dramatic wording unless it's false. If it's false, swap in the closest true version and flag it in one line. Fouquet was imprisoned for life, not killed, so "you will get killed" became "you could lose everything". Commenters fact-check history.
- Check dates and outcomes before writing them.
  - Law 1 (Fouquet): the party was at Vaux-le-Vicomte, 17 Aug 1661; the arrest came 19 days later; Fouquet died in prison in 1680; Louis XIV reused Le Vau, Le Nôtre and Le Brun for Versailles.
  - Law 2 (Jobs): Sculley joined Apple in 1983; Jobs was pushed out in 1985 and came back in 1997; Microsoft put $150M into Apple in 1997.
  - Law 3 (Conceal Your Intentions; Bill Gates / 86-DOS): IBM signed with Microsoft in Nov 1980, before Microsoft had an OS. Microsoft licensed 86-DOS from Seattle Computer Products in Dec 1980 for $25k, then bought all rights in July 1981 for $50k ($75k total), without telling them the customer was IBM. Microsoft kept the right to license DOS to other PC makers. The IBM PC launched in Aug 1981. SCP sued and settled in 1986 for about $1M. Gates was Forbes #1 from 1995. IBM did NOT pay "billions": it paid a small fee. The money came from licensing DOS to every clone maker.
  - Law 4 (Say Less): the user asked for a different example than Kanye, so the replacement is **Gerald Ratner**. He gave a speech to about 6,000 business people at the Institute of Directors, Royal Albert Hall, on 23 Apr 1991. In it he called a £4.95 sherry decanter set "total crap" and said the earrings were cheaper than an M&S prawn sandwich, which "will probably last longer". The company had about 2,000 shops and roughly 50% of the UK market. Around £500M was wiped off its value. He was fired in Nov 1992, and the company was renamed Signet in Sep 1993. "Doing a Ratner" became a phrase.
  - Law 4 original (Kanye West, swapped out): weeks of antisemitic remarks in Oct 2022, including saying on Drink Champs that Adidas couldn't drop him. Adidas ended the deal on 25 Oct 2022, and Forbes took him off the billionaire list the same day. The Adidas deal was worth $1.5B, leaving him at $400M. Gap, Balenciaga and CAA also cut ties. Paraphrase his words on camera; never quote the slur part.
  - Law 5 (Reputation): the user asked for a different example than SBF, so the replacement is **Tiger Woods**. Forbes named him the first billion-dollar athlete in 2009. On 27 Nov 2009 he had a car crash outside his home and the affairs came out. On 11 Dec he admitted it and took a break from golf. Accenture dropped him on 13 Dec, then AT&T and Gatorade; Nike stayed. A UC Davis study found his sponsors' shareholders lost $5-12B. After the 2008 US Open he didn't win another major for 11 years (until the 2019 Masters).
  - Law 5 original (Sam Bankman-Fried vs CZ, swapped out): FTX was valued at $32B (Jan 2022). A CoinDesk article ran on 2 Nov 2022. CZ tweeted on 6 Nov that Binance would sell its FTT. $6B was withdrawn in 72 hours, and FTX filed for bankruptcy on 11 Nov. SBF got 25 years (Mar 2024). The fraud was real, so keep the line that he'd used customers' money, which keeps the story defensible.
  - Law 6 (Court Attention at All Costs; the book's examples Barnum, Lola Montez and Mata Hari are off limits). The first draft used **Banksy**: *Girl with Balloon* sold for £1,042,000 at Sotheby's London on 5 Oct 2018 and shredded itself halfway; renamed *Love Is in the Bin*, it resold on 14 Oct 2021 for £18,582,000 (about 17.8×). People said it was a stunt or that the auction house was in on it. He has never confirmed his identity, so say "never shown his face". The user chose **Conor McGregor** for the "better hated than ignored" hook:
    - April 2013: "180 euros a week off the social welfare", days before his UFC debut.
    - March 2015: he grabbed Aldo's belt at the Dublin press conference.
    - UFC 194, 12 Dec 2015: he knocked out Aldo in 13 seconds.
    - 26 Aug 2017: Mayweather (49-0) fight, his pro boxing debut. He lost by TKO in round 10.
    - Money: $30M guaranteed; "around $100M" is his own figure with PPV (reported by Forbes), so keep the word "around".
    - Flag that a civil jury found him liable for sexual assault in 2024 (appeal lost 2025); comments may bring it up.
    - "Love me or hate me… you're gonna watch me" is Mayweather's line, not McGregor's.
  - Law 7 (Get Others to Do the Work, but Always Take the Credit; the book's Tesla/Edison story is off limits). The first draft used **Walt Disney / Ub Iwerks**:
    - Friends and partners since Kansas City, 1919.
    - Ub drew the final Mickey design and animated *Plane Crazy* (May 1928) alone, mostly behind a locked door, at about 700 drawings a day (Ub's own words).
    - Early credits read "A Walt Disney Comic by Ub Iwerks", so never say Ub got zero credit. Say the world remembered one name.
    - Ub quit in Jan 1930 and sold his 20% for $2,920 ("about $3,000"). Jeff Ryan estimates that stake would be worth ~$32B today, so say "billions".
    - In 1932 Walt got an honorary Oscar "for the creation of Mickey Mouse". His record is 26 Oscars total, 22 competitive.
    - Ub came back in 1940 as an employee.
    - "It was all started by a mouse" is a real Walt line (1954).
- When the law is a strategy (conceal, use a decoy), the story's key beat is the concealed move itself ("he never told them who the buyer was"). Give it its own line in the script, because that line is what proves the law. If he skips it on camera, a card carries it in the edit.
- He uses the script as a loose guide and improvises on camera. Fillers, new phrasing and sometimes a wrong number or claim come in. The edit follows what he actually said (see talking-head-tiktok-edit, 48 Laws section, for how false claims are handled).
- Keep the claims defensible. Write "his enemy helped save the company", not "the friend almost killed it".
