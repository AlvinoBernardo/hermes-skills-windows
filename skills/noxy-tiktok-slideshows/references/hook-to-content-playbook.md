## What makes the CONTENT work
1. Fulfill the cover's exact promise. Write a one-line internal promise before any slides: who it is for, outcome, count, kind of advice. A 'simple changes' cover requires easy concrete changes; a 'skinny fat to lean' cover requires muscle/recomposition advice; a transformation hook requires actual changes behind the claimed result. Never merely replace a cover over a recycled script.
2. Pay off the hook immediately. Pictures 2–3 should give recognizable, useful actions with an example, not definitions, introductory filler or generic encouragement. Explain necessary context concisely or in caption; do not bury the practical payoff.
3. Use one distinct idea per tip. Each numbered heading names a clear action. Support it with useful explanation and a concrete example, following the fuller-copy preference below. Avoid two slides that collapse into the same advice (e.g. follow a plan / repeat workouts / track lifts) unless they teach genuinely distinct steps needed by the hook.
4. Prefer specific everyday examples to abstract wellness language. 'Order less syrup' + 'Try half the syrup in your usual coffee' is clearer than 'make healthier choices'. 'Walk after lunch' is clearer than 'make walks a ritual'. 'Include cooking oil in your log' is clearer than a generic lecture about awareness.
5. Use the latest preference: roughly 25 useful words per tip body, with more only when the topic or a necessary example/qualification earns it. This supersedes the older 26–35-word default and keep-the-length instruction. The user's own examples are action + practical method/example + why: protein foods for fullness/muscle support; air frying or measuring oil with a teaspoon while retaining enjoyable food. Match that natural explanatory voice, not terse slogans or artificial word-count padding. A modestly longer example can be better than a short instruction with its reasoning removed. Avoid unsupported superlatives and guaranteed muscle-gain or weight-loss claims. Keep plain headings, established font size, and no caption dependency. Historical successful-post screenshot lengths remain observations, not mandates or proof of performance. View-time priority does not justify filler.
6. Give viewers a reason for the next swipe through useful non-redundant ideas and a clear count, not artificial cliffhangers. Deliver exactly the promised number of useful numbered tips. The restored standalone Noxy CTA is an additional unnumbered slide between tips, not one of the promised tips. On conversion from integrated CTA, retain a useful numbered advice slide and move the product mention/card to its own slide. Do not promote Noxy twice.

## Internal pre-render outline
Hook (exact) -> promise -> tip 1 + example + image -> remaining distinct tips -> relevant Noxy bridge -> ending.
Run these checks before rendering:
- Does each tip answer THIS hook, not merely the broad category of weight loss?
- Do pictures 2–3 deliver a concrete action rather than generic theory?
- Does the numbered-tip count match the promise?
- Are any two tips redundant? Can any slide be removed without losing value?
- Is every heading simple, actionable and distinct from its explanation?
- Is the Noxy mention connected to the adjacent tip? Are personal claims supported?
- Do all photographs fit both topic and male/female selection?
- Is this materially different from recent exports, not just renamed copy?
- Are advice, numbers and product claims accurate, without invented results?
